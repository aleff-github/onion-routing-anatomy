The story

The project began as study and became, over time, a technical book with its own laboratories. This page tells how.

Where it comes from

While working on my bachelor's thesis, I started to look at Tor below the level at which it is usually described: not how to use it, but what actually travels between relays — cells, circuits, keys, and the algorithms that hold them together.

My interest did not end with the degree. In the following years I kept studying the material in more depth, reorganising and extending it.

From a thesis to a book

Over time the goal changed. Understanding Tor was no longer enough for me: the aim became a technical work able to explain it concretely, down to the protocol, the cells, the circuits and the algorithms, with every statement tied to the specifications and to the source code.

Why laboratories

Describing a mechanism does not show that it works as described. The laboratories come from that need: a private Tor network running on a single computer, where each step in the text can be reproduced, measured and checked. They are now part of the book: twenty of them, each adding a piece to a small educational Tor client.

Why it is free

I decided to give priority to the free circulation of knowledge. The whole project — the book, its LaTeX sources, the code and the laboratories — is freely available, so that anyone can study it, reproduce it and improve it. The book is free of charge; what can be done with it is set by its license, the GNU GPL.

How the current text was produced, and which part AI tools played in it, is described on the AI transparency page.
